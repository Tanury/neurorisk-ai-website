Literature Survey

What existing research has done.

Literature Survey

Brain imaging research for AD, PD and MS still has clear limitations. Most deep learning models target a single disease and are trained on one public cohort such as ADNI, so they often generalise poorly to scans from other hospitals, scanners and acquisition protocols [2]. Labelled 3D scans are scarce, which is why many studies depend on residual networks and transfer learning from medical pre-training sets to limit overfitting [1], [3]. Early changes in PD are subtle on structural MRI, and MS models usually rely on lesion masks that vary between annotators and rarely include healthy controls. As a result, most imaging work detects established disease rather than early risk.

Retinal OCT has emerged as a low-cost window into the central nervous system. Thinning of inner retinal layers is linked to multiple sclerosis and other neurodegenerative conditions, and public datasets with manual layer delineations now exist [4].

For brain signals, compact deep learning models such as EEGNet allow efficient EEG analysis, but most studies focus on a single neurological disease [5]. Subject-level data leakage, where recordings from the same person appear in both training and test sets, can also inflate reported accuracy, which highlights the need for subject-independent evaluation [6].

Fluid biomarkers such as neurofilament light chain (NfL) reflect neuronal damage, but a single marker can change across several neurological disorders. The literature therefore stresses combining biomarkers with clinical information to support disease assessment [7].

Speech also carries measurable markers of neurological disease [8], yet it is rarely included in multimodal neurological risk models.

Most published models still use a single modality and a single disease. Where multimodal work exists, it often assumes every input is present for every patient, which does not reflect clinical practice, although methods such as M3Care show that learning with missing modalities is possible [9].

Explainability methods such as Grad-CAM make CNN predictions easier to check [10], and approaches such as SHAPE measure how much each modality contributes to a multimodal prediction [11]. Missing-modality learning, uncertainty quantification and explainability are each well developed, but they have largely been studied in isolation and are rarely brought together in one end-to-end diagnostic tool.

Research Gap

What is still missing in existing work.

Research Gap

Despite recent progress, existing work still has clear limitations:

  • Few systems cover more than one neurodegenerative disease in one pipeline.
  • Imaging, EEG and biomarker models are developed separately with no shared output format.
  • Fusion methods rarely handle patients with missing modalities.
  • Explanations are often missing or not shown in a form clinicians can use.

These gaps point to the need for one system that combines modalities, covers several diseases and explains its results.

Research Problem

The problem this research addresses.

The Problem

Neurodegenerative diseases are usually diagnosed after symptoms appear, when much of the damage is already done. Each test (a MRI scan, an OCT, an EEG, a blood panel) is read on its own, by a different specialist, and no single test is reliable enough for early detection. Useful signals are spread across modalities and rarely combined.

Our Approach

Each modality has its own encoder that produces a fixed-size embedding. An adaptive fusion engine combines whatever embeddings are available into a risk score for each disease, and Grad-CAM and feature-impact views show which regions and inputs drove each result.

Research Question

How can brain and retinal imaging, EEG and fluid biomarker data be combined into one explainable early-risk assessment for AD, PD and MS, even when some of these inputs are missing?

Research Objectives

The main objective and each member's specific objectives.

Main Objective

Build an explainable multimodal system that estimates early risk of Alzheimer's Disease, Parkinson's Disease and Multiple Sclerosis from brain and retinal imaging, EEG, voice recordings and fluid biomarker data.

Dissanayaka D. M. T. M

Vision (Imaging) Encoder

  • Build image encoders for brain MRI, DaTscan SPECT and retinal OCT that pair each disease with its most suitable modality
  • Output a 256-dimensional L2-normalised embedding from every branch through a shared output head for multimodal integration
  • Provide Grad-CAM maps and visual overlays so clinicians can see which regions of the scan drove each prediction
Sudugodage S. R

EEG Encoder

  • Extract features from EEG recordings linked to early neurodegeneration
  • Train an EEG encoder that outputs embeddings in the shared format
  • Evaluate if the learned embeddings effectively capture patterns that help distinguish healthy controls from individuals with neurodegenerative conditions.
Danishiya Appulingam

Fluid Biomarker Encoder

  • Model fluid biomarker values associated with each disease
  • Produce biomarker embeddings for the fusion engine
  • Evaluate the effectiveness of the biomarker embeddings in capturing relevant information for early risk assessment.
Aarabhi A. Y

Fusion Engine

  • Combine available embeddings with an adaptive fusion method
  • Output per-disease risk scores that work with missing inputs
  • Show each modality’s probabilities, its weight and contribution, and an agreement label (Strong, Partial or Discordant), so a clinician can recompute the fused score.

The system has four layers. Inputs are processed by a separate encoder per modality, the fusion engine turns the combined embeddings into risk scores and explanations, and a FastAPI backend serves results to the dashboard.

Component Methods

Vision (Imaging) Encoder Dissanayaka D. M. T. M

Five pathology-matched branches, one per disease and imaging type, sharing one output head that gives the same 256-d embedding.

  • AD: dual-stream 3D and 2D ResNet-18 on T1 MRI from ADNI (AD, MCI and CN)
  • PD: 2D CNN on a specific-binding-ratio slice of DaTscan SPECT from PPMI, with a supplementary structural MRI branch
  • MS (MRI): MindGlide segmentation, then five region volumes normalised by total brain volume (MS3SEG with LEMON controls)
  • MS (OCT): multilayer perceptron on eight retinal layer thicknesses from the JHU OCT dataset
  • Evaluation: held-out test sets for ADNI and PPMI; leave-one-out cross-validation for the small MS cohorts

EEG Encoder Sudugodage S. R

Turns raw resting-state EEG into a 256-d embedding with separate AD, PD and MS risk scores.

  • Data: OpenNeuro AD/FTD/HC and UC San Diego PD resting-state EEG, giving a 115-subject reference cohort
  • Preprocessing: common 19-channel montage, 0.5–40 Hz band-pass filter, automated FastICA artifact removal and 4-second epochs with amplitude rejection
  • Model: hybrid STFT and Morlet wavelet time–frequency inputs to an encoder with a shared projection head and L2 normalisation
  • Evaluation: subject-grouped five-fold cross-validation with an automated leakage check
  • Returns a zero vector and an availability flag when EEG is missing or fails quality control

Fluid Biomarker Encoder Danishiya Appulingam

Interprets fluid biomarkers together with demographic, clinical, functional and genetic information.

  • Data: combined 24-feature dataset of 10,000 records, split 7,000 / 1,500 / 1,500
  • Features: eight fluid biomarkers (Aβ42/40, total tau, p-tau181, NfL, GFAP, α-synuclein, GDF15, CRP) with demographic, cognitive, sleep, motor, functional and genetic variables
  • Preprocessing: median imputation with missingness indicators, standardisation and one-hot encoding in scikit-learn pipelines; values can be extracted from lab reports for user review
  • Models: Extra Trees for the disease pattern (AD, Healthy, MS, PD) and Logistic Regression for the Low, Medium or High risk level, chosen by macro F1
  • Evaluation: 96.33% pattern accuracy and 95.53% risk-level accuracy on 1,500 held-out records

Fusion Engine Aarabhi A. Y

Combines the voice, biomedical and EEG outputs into one risk level per disease.

  • Fusion approach: quality-weighted late fusion; each modality's output becomes evidence for each disease, is weighted by input quality and averaged into a Low, Moderate or High risk level
  • Missing modalities: poor-quality inputs get less weight and missing biomarker values are imputed and counted; the current version needs voice, biomedical and EEG for the same participant, and a masked-attention engine for any subset was designed but not built
  • Explainability and evaluation: the dashboard shows each modality's probabilities, weight, contribution and an agreement label (Strong, Partial or Discordant); reviewed by the clinical supervisor, but not yet evaluated against outcomes because no dataset has more than one modality for the same people
